## Q1. Verified status (as of Nov 2026) of AI Act timelines, the Digital Omnibus on AI, the GPAI Code of Practice, and AI Act enforcement

### Takeaway
The Digital Omnibus on AI is real and in force. It is Regulation (EU) 2026/1744: published in the OJ on 24 July 2026, in force 27 July 2026. It moves Annex III (standalone) high-risk obligations to 2 Dec 2027 and Annex I (product-embedded) high-risk obligations to 2 Aug 2028. It also adds new Article 5 prohibitions (nudification/CSAM tools) from 2 Dec 2026. The Commission's GPAI enforcement powers apply from 2 Aug 2026. Trackers as of late Sept/early Oct 2026 report **no published AI Act fine** by the Commission or any national authority. I found no confirmed formal enforcement decision after that date.

### Inferences
- The brief's description ("Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, delaying high-risk obligations to Dec 2027") is **correct**, with one refinement: Dec 2027 applies to Annex III systems, and Annex I systems move to Aug 2028.
- With no fines as of Oct 2026 and high-risk duties deferred, the AI Act is in a "law on the books / not yet in action" phase. Enforcement-focused student papers would therefore have to analyse institutional design, guidance and soft law (Codes, guidelines, standards) rather than case law.
- The Omnibus itself (deregulation, "simplification," competitiveness narrative after the Draghi report) is a fresh 2026 topic. Peer-reviewed analysis of the *final* text will be thin, because publication lags.

- I could not confirm whether any formal AI Office proceeding (request for information, Art. 92 evaluation) or national Article 5 decision occurred between Oct and 11 Nov 2026.
- The official Commission list of GPAI Code signatories was not accessed.

## Q2. Main scholarly debates on the AI Act: authors, articles, journals

### Takeaway
The legal literature on the AI Act is very large and very active. It has four main lines:
- critiques of the product-safety/New Legislative Framework architecture and its weak protection of fundamental rights;
- whether the Act is truly risk-based;
- delegation of normative choices to private standardisation (CEN-CENELEC JTC 21);
- regulation of general-purpose/generative AI and the governance bodies (AI Office, Board, national authorities).

The Omnibus has now opened a fifth line: "simplification" as deregulation.

## Q3. The Brussels effect debate and whether other jurisdictions copy or diverge (UK, Canada, Brazil, South Korea, China, India); is there empirical diffusion work?

### Takeaway
The scholarly consensus has moved from "likely Brussels effect" (2022) to scepticism (2024–2026). The current reading is a partial de facto effect at most, a weak de jure effect, and parallels better explained by shared OECD-style diffusion or experimentalism.

On the ground, jurisdictions are mixed:
- **Partial convergence:** Brazil's bill (risk tiers, still not law), South Korea's AI Basic Act (in force Jan 2026, much lighter), and China's labelling rules (parallel to Article 50 but state-driven).
- **Divergence:** UK (no AI Act), Canada (AIDA died), India (voluntary guidelines).

Systematic empirical tracking of de jure diffusion is **largely missing**. This is the clearest gap for a student paper.

### Inferences
- Labelling and transparency rules for synthetic content (EU Art. 50, China's 2025 Measures, Korea's labelling duty) are the clearest area of **substantive convergence**. That convergence predates the AI Act in China's case (2022 deep synthesis rules), which complicates any "Brussels" attribution. A comparative paper on AI-content labelling across the EU, China, Korea (and Brazil's draft) is feasible from public sources and under-studied.
- The Omnibus delay may weaken the Brussels effect further, because the "first mover" now applies its high-risk regime later than Korea's law entered into force. This is a researchable, novel claim, but it is my inference.
- Canada's AIDA failure and the UK's non-legislation are useful "negative cases" for diffusion theory. They are under-used in the literature as far as this search could see.

### Gaps
- **I found no systematic, dataset-driven empirical study** measuring legislative text diffusion from the AI Act, for example text-reuse or provision-coding across countries. The Digital Society paper itself describes its evidence base as limited. This is a strong gap for an original student paper: code AI bills from OECD.AI's policy database against AI Act provisions.
- I could not verify the authors of the Digital Society 2026 article.
- I did not verify primary texts for Korea (Enforcement Decree) or Brazil (Chamber record).

## Q4. GDPR as an AI enforcement tool (Italian Garante: OpenAI, DeepSeek, Replika/Luka, Character.AI; EDPB)

### Takeaway
While AI Act enforcement is dormant, the GDPR has been the **actual** enforcement tool against AI in Europe, and the Italian Garante is the most aggressive actor. A major 2026 twist is that the Court of Rome **annulled** the €15m OpenAI fine in March 2026 on **competence (one-stop-shop) grounds**, not the merits. That raises a live, under-analysed question about whether national DPAs can police non-EU AI firms once they establish an EU base.

### Cited Findings
- **OpenAI**
  - Temporary restriction in March 2023. Decision of 2 Nov 2024, publicised 20 Dec 2024: €15m fine plus a 6-month awareness campaign.
  - On 21 Mar 2025 the court suspended the campaign and conditionally suspended the fine.
  - On **18 Mar 2026 the Court of Rome annulled the decision in full**. OpenAI had established in Ireland in Feb 2024, before the decision, so the Irish DPC was the lead authority and the Garante lacked competence. Legal basis, transparency and age-verification merits were not decided. A Cassation appeal is possible.
  - Sources: [Wilson Sonsini](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/openai-prevails-in-landmark-italian-ai-and-gdpr-enforcement-case.html); [gdprfine.com](https://gdprfine.com/cases/openai-15-million-garante-2024); [ScienceDirect overview citing the decision dates](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2212473X26000416)
- **DeepSeek:** the Garante ordered a block/limitation in Italy in January 2025. — [gdprfine.com Garante page](https://gdprfine.com/dpa/italy-garante) (secondary)
- **Replika (Luka Inc.):** a 2023 processing ban (age verification, privacy notice). In 2025 a **€5m fine** for lacking a valid legal basis and having no effective age verification. The fine was announced May 2025. — [gdprfine.com](https://gdprfine.com/dpa/italy-garante); [privacyterms.io tracker](https://privacyterms.io/ai-privacy-fines-enforcement-tracker) (secondary)
- **Character.AI (Character Technologies Inc.):** Garante decision no. 487 of **3 July 2026**, press release 9 July 2026, **€158,000 fine**.
  - Violations: late EU representative (Art. 27), information gaps (Arts 12–14), inadequate DPIA (Art. 35), security and privacy by design/default (Arts 24–25), and minors' protection and age verification (self-declaration only).
  - Orders: fix age verification, make the cooling-off for blocked minors effective, and set minors' profiles private by default, all within 120 days.
  - Mitigating factors: negligence rather than intent, and cooperation.
  - Sources: [Garante press release (docweb 10269594)](https://www.garanteprivacy.it/home/docweb/-/docweb-display/docweb/10269594); [Federprivacy](https://www.federprivacy.org/informazione/garante-privacy/il-garante-privacy-sanziona-character-ai-per-violazioni-del-gdpr); [provvedimentigaranteprivacy.it](https://provvedimentigaranteprivacy.it/provvedimenti/character-technologies-10269571/)
- **EDPB Opinion 28/2024 (17 Dec 2024, requested by the Irish DPC):**
  - AI models trained on personal data are not automatically anonymous. Anonymity requires an "insignificant" likelihood of extraction.
  - Legitimate interest is possible under a three-step test.
  - Downstream deployers have their own obligations.
  - Scholarly overview: "The AI Gospel according to the EDPB" (ScienceDirect, 2026); Velli in *European Papers*.
  - Sources: [EDPB](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/opinion-board-art-64/opinion-282024-certain-data-protection-aspects_en); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2212473X26000416); [European Papers](https://www.europeanpapers.eu/system/files/pdf_version/EP_EF_2024_H_004_Federica_Velli_00822.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Strong student topic, moderate heat.** The OpenAI annulment (Mar 2026) and the Character.AI decision (Jul 2026) are too recent to be absorbed by peer-reviewed scholarship. A paper on "The one-stop-shop as a shield: national DPA competence over generative AI after *OpenAI v Garante*" would be original and can be built entirely from public decisions.
- The Garante's pattern shows that the GDPR is functioning as de facto AI child-safety regulation: Replika and Character.AI both turn on age verification. This connects to the Omnibus's new nudification/CSAM bans and the DSA.
- Because the Omnibus postponed high-risk duties, the GDPR remains the primary enforceable instrument against AI harms in the EU through at least 2027.

### Gaps
- The full Court of Rome judgment and the DeepSeek and Replika decisions were not opened. Dates and amounts come from secondary trackers, except Character.AI, whose primary press-release URL was located.
- I did not check whether the Garante appealed to the Cassazione.
- I did not search for other DPAs' AI actions (Irish DPC on X/Grok and Meta AI training, Dutch AP, CNIL).

## Q5. Global South / African AI governance: volume of scholarship and what is missing

### Takeaway
Scholarship is **growing but thin and mostly non-peer-reviewed**. It consists largely of think-tank commentary, preprints and index reports, with only a few journal articles (e.g. *Global Policy* 2026). The recurring themes are:
- data colonialism / digital sovereignty;
- the gap between the AU Continental AI Strategy's ambitions and implementation;
- US–China competition;
- the non-binding nature of most frameworks.

Doctrinal legal analysis of actual African AI-relevant law (data protection acts, national AI strategies or bills) is notably scarce. This makes the area a strong, under-served topic.

### Cited Findings
- The **AU Continental AI Strategy** was adopted July 2024. It addresses "AI colonialism" and data sovereignty and is tied to the AU Data Policy Framework (2022). — [regulations.ai record](https://regulations.ai/regulations/RAI-XA-NA-CONTINE-2024); [Wilson Center](https://www.wilsoncenter.org/blog-post/regulating-artificial-intelligence-africa-strategies-and-insights-kenya-ghana-and-african)
- **"From Aspiration to Implementation: The AU AI Continental Strategy and the Development-Governance Paradox"** (working paper). It assesses the first 18 months (July 2024 to Oct 2025) and finds investment concentrated in four countries, with governance "largely aspirational." Only the summary was seen; not peer-reviewed. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/396230260_From_Aspiration_to_Implementation_The_African_Union_AI_Continental_Strategy_and_the_Development-Governance_Paradox)
- **Noesselt, "Fragmented Global AI Governance? Chinese AI Leadership Dreams and Africa's Quest for Digital Autonomy," *Global Policy* (2026).** Peer-reviewed. Frames data sovereignty as resistance to digital colonialism. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1758-5899.70199)
- **National AI strategies:** 22 of 54 African countries had one as of 2026, up from 16 in 2024. This is commentary-sourced and counts vary. — [Horn Review, May 2026](https://hornreview.org/2026/05/22/artificial-intelligence-data-sovereignty-and-the-african-union/)
- **South Africa study.** The AU instruments are "useful but not directly enforceable" in domestic law. ResearchGate preprint. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/415267701_Data_Colonisation_and_AI_Governance_in_South_Africa_Data_Protection_Algorithmic_Power_and_the_Struggle_for_Digital_Sovereignty)
- **Global Index on Responsible AI**
  - 1st edition (June 2024): 138 countries, including 41 African countries. Non-state actors were active in 30 African countries.
  - 2nd edition (2026): **78% of Global South responsible-AI frameworks are non-binding vs 42% in the Global North.**
  - [Global Center on AI Governance](https://globalcenter.ai/news/ai-governance-fails-to-deliver-global-index-on-responsible-ai-reveals-critical-gaps); [GCG Africa civil society](https://www.globalcenter.ai/research/the-outsized-role-of-african-civil-society-and-universities-in-advancing-responsible-ai); [EU-Africa Chamber on 2nd edition](https://eu-africa-chamber.org/artificial-intelligence-ai-is-advancing-faster-than-governments-can-protect-people-new-global-index-finds/)
- **Latin America:** Central and South American countries generally score below 50/100 on the Index. — [Global Solutions Journal 2025](https://www.global-solutions-initiative.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/v8_WS_GS_journal_11_S156-163.pdf)
- **Under-representation is documented.**
  - A meta-review of 200 AI ethics/governance documents (2014–2022) found that under 4.5% came from South America, Africa and Oceania combined. Only 4.5% proposed legally binding regulation. — [TechXplore summary of *Patterns* 2023 study](https://techxplore.com/news/2023-10-global-consensus-ethical-ai.pdf). Authors are Corrêa et al. **[background, unverified here]**.
  - Roche, Lewis & Wall (2021) found Global South and gender perspectives scarcely referenced. — [DeepAI listing](https://deepai.org/publication/artificial-intelligence-ethics-an-inclusive-global-discourse)
  - Governance policies "predominantly encode a Global North perspective." — [PMC9540088](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9540088)
- **Igarapé Institute (2025), "Governance Gaps" database.** Shows a widening North–South divide in AI policy initiatives. — [Igarapé PDF](https://igarape.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/CI_EN_GFB-Governance-Gaps-Assessing-Global-Disparities-in-Al-Policy-Initiatives-Around-the-World.pdf)
- **"The Global Majority in International AI Governance" (arXiv 2601.17191, 2026).** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.17191)
- **Georgetown Journal of International Affairs.** On US–China AI competition in Africa: labour exploitation, data colonialism, digital authoritarianism. — [GJIA](https://gjia.georgetown.edu/global-governance/the-united-states-china-and-ai-competition-in-africa-lessons-for-the-global-south/)
- **Center for Data Innovation (industry-linked).** Argues EU rule-export burdens Global South countries. Opinion, not empirical. — [datainnovation.org](https://datainnovation.org/?p=22696)
- Png, "At the Tensions of South and North: Critical Roles of Global South Stakeholders in AI Governance" (FAccT 2022) and Arun, "AI and the Global South: Designing for Other Worlds" (OUP Handbook, 2020) are commonly cited anchors. **[background, unverified here]**

### Inferences
- Heat level: **rising, under-supplied**. The policy and commentary volume is high, but peer-reviewed legal analysis is low. The gaps this literature names itself are:
  - the implementation gap (strategies are aspirational, frameworks non-binding);
  - the lack of enforceability of AU instruments in domestic law;
  - thin empirical evidence;
  - Northern framing.
- **Feasible student projects:**
  - a doctrinal comparison of how African data protection authorities (Kenya ODPC, Nigeria NDPC, South Africa Information Regulator) have or have not applied data-protection law to AI, i.e. the "GDPR-as-AI-tool" question transplanted to Africa;
  - a coded comparison of the 22 African national AI strategies against the AU Strategy and the EU AI Act, a Brussels-effect test in the Global South.

### Gaps
- I did not verify any binding African AI statute or bill as of Nov 2026, nor Kenya's or Nigeria's draft AI bills/strategies.
- I did not verify the AU Strategy primary text (au.int) or any AU implementation report.
- The 22/54 strategy count comes from a single commentary source.

## Q6. Explicit research gaps and calls for future research identified by scholars (and suitability for a student paper)

### Takeaway
Scholars explicitly flag four gaps:
1. thin empirical evidence on AI Act diffusion (Digital Society 2026);
2. a mismatch between management-system standards and substantive risk content, plus the legitimacy of private standardisation (EJRR; ICT Law 2025);
3. un-addressed immaterial and societal harms in liability (Wachter);
4. an implementation and enforceability gap in Global South frameworks (GIRAI; AU Strategy working paper).

Post-Omnibus and post-*OpenAI v Garante*, new unanswered questions concern deregulation and DPA competence.

### Cited Findings
- The Digital Society 2026 article calls its evidence base limited and gauges the Brussels effect "under uncertainty." That is an implicit call for empirical diffusion work. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44206-026-00284-0)
- Management-system standards govern *whether* risk management happens, not *which* risks. Paid access and pending litigation leave the legitimacy question open. — [T&F 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13600834.2025.2570966); [EJRR](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/european-journal-of-risk-regulation/article/european-ai-standards-technical-standardisation-and-implementation-challenges-under-the-eu-ai-act/E5157BA0391FFA9E1A3233E636005192)
- *Mapping the Regulatory Learning Space for the EU AI Act* (arXiv 2503.05787) frames AI Act implementation as needing structured regulatory learning, which is a call for implementation research. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.05787)
- Wachter proposes regulatory mechanisms to close loopholes in self-certification and in immaterial harm. — [YJoLT](https://yjolt.org/node/1112)
- 78% of Global South frameworks are non-binding, and AU instruments are not domestically enforceable. — [GCG](https://eu-africa-chamber.org/artificial-intelligence-ai-is-advancing-faster-than-governments-can-protect-people-new-global-index-finds/); [ResearchGate SA study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/415267701_Data_Colonisation_and_AI_Governance_in_South_Africa_Data_Protection_Algorithmic_Power_and_the_Struggle_for_Digital_Sovereignty)
