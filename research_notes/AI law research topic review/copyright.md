# AI and Copyright (Training Data, Fair Use, Outputs): Litigation and Scholarship, 2023–2026

Research date: 2026-11-11. Method: web search across legal press, law-firm alerts, court-document mirrors (CourtListener, govinfo), the ChatGPTiseatingtheworld tracker, copyright.gov, and SSRN/arXiv/Science. Several law-firm sites (akingump.com, lowenstein.com) could not be fetched from this session, so some findings rest on search snippets rather than full-text reading. Those points are flagged. ## Q1. What is the verified status (as of November 2026) of the major cases and rulings?

### Takeaway
The field has moved from pleadings to merits. There is now (a) one federal appellate fair-use ruling on AI training (Third Circuit, Thomson Reuters v. Ross, Sept. 2026: not fair use, but for a non-generative tool), (b) two split N.D. Cal. district rulings from June 2025 (Bartz, Kadrey) that favoured training as fair use while condemning or leaving open piracy-sourced acquisition, (c) a record $1.5B class settlement (Bartz, final approval July 2026), (d) summary-judgment briefing in the NYT/OpenAI MDL that closed on Nov. 6, 2026, with no ruling yet, and (e) divergent first-instance European outcomes (UK Getty: no secondary infringement, on appeal; Germany GEMA: infringement through memorisation, on appeal). The human-authorship rule is settled for fully autonomous output (cert denied in Thaler, March 2026). The EU AI Act's GPAI copyright duties have applied since Aug. 2025, and Commission enforcement powers have applied since Aug. 2026.

### Cited Findings

**Bartz v. Anthropic (N.D. Cal., No. 3:24-cv-05417)**
- Judge Alsup ruled at summary judgment in June 2025 that training LLMs on lawfully acquired books was fair use ("exceedingly transformative"), but that downloading and keeping pirated copies (LibGen, PiLiMi) in a central library was not excused. Class certification and a settlement followed. The date and holding are from my background knowledge and are confirmed indirectly by the settlement coverage below. The opinion should be pulled from CourtListener for exact citation. [Authors Guild](https://authorsguild.org/news/court-grants-final-approval-anthropic-copyright-settlement/)
- The $1.5B settlement received **final approval on July 20, 2026**, and final judgment was entered. It is described as the largest copyright class settlement in US history: about $3,100 per work for roughly 480,000+ works downloaded from LibGen and PiLiMi. — [Authors Guild](https://authorsguild.org/news/court-grants-final-approval-anthropic-copyright-settlement/); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/anthropic-authors-1-5-billion-deal-receives-final-approval); [Pearl Cohen](https://pearlcohen.com/federal-court-approves-1-5-billion-anthropic-copyright-settlement-largest-in-history)
- Reported details: a 91.3% claims rate; 350 valid opt-outs (1,802 works, 0.03%); all 53 objections overruled; fees cut from the requested 12.5% to about 6.8%; service awards cut from $50k to $15k. The release covers only past acquisition and copying through Aug. 25, 2025. **Output claims and future conduct are not released.** — [TAA blog](https://blog.taaonline.net/2026/07/bartz-v-anthropic-granted-final-approval-by-the-court/); [Authors Guild](https://authorsguild.org/news/court-grants-final-approval-anthropic-copyright-settlement/)
- UNCERTAIN: the search summary attributes final approval to **Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín**, not Alsup. This would be consistent with reassignment after Alsup's reported move to inactive status, but I did not verify it. The docket prefix also varies across sources (3:24 vs 4:24). Expense reimbursement is reported as about $2.6M in some sources and $3M in others. — [Pearl Cohen](https://pearlcohen.com/federal-court-approves-1-5-billion-anthropic-copyright-settlement-largest-in-history)

**Kadrey v. Meta (N.D. Cal., No. 3:23-cv-03417)**
- The summary-judgment ruling for Meta on fair use is reported at 788 F. Supp. 3d 1028 (N.D. Cal. 2025) (Chhabria, J., June 2025). It was narrow: plaintiffs lost because they failed to develop market-harm (including "market dilution") evidence, not because training is categorically fair use. The Copyright Office's own fair-use index summarises it. — [copyright.gov fair use summary](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/Kadrey-v-Meta-Platforms-Inc-788-F-Supp-3d-1028-ND-Cal-2025.pdf)
- The live remainder is the **torrenting/distribution claim** (reproduction, making available, and distribution while torrenting). In early April 2026, Judge Chhabria allowed a fourth amended complaint adding contributory infringement. Meta won partial SJ on the DMCA claim. — [ailawwiki (secondary)](https://ailawwiki.com/News_Kadrey_v_Meta_Fourth_Amended_Complaint_April_2026); [CourtListener docket](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.415175/gov.uscourts.cand.415175.661.0.pdf)
- Schedule (single source, not docket-verified): expert discovery closes Nov. 20, 2026; plaintiffs' opening SJ brief is due Dec. 11, 2026; the **SJ hearing on distribution is set for Feb. 25, 2027**. — [ChatGPTiseatingtheworld](https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2026/03/27/kadrey-v-meta-gets-scheduling-order-summary-judgment-motion-hearing-for-distribution-claim-wont-be-heard-until-feb-25-2027/)

**Thomson Reuters v. Ross Intelligence (D. Del. → 3d Cir.)**
- District court (Bibas, J., sitting by designation, Feb. 11, 2025): Westlaw headnotes are copyrightable, and Ross's use to train a non-generative legal search tool was not fair use. An interlocutory appeal was certified. — [Business Law Today (ABA)](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/business-law-today/2026-september/thomson-reuters-v-ross-one-year-later/)
- The Third Circuit heard oral argument on **June 11, 2026**. — [ChatGPTiseatingtheworld](https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2026/04/14/third-circuit-sets-oral-argument-on-june-11-for-1st-appeal-of-decision-on-fair-use-in-ai-training-case-follows-another-recent-third-circuit-decision-on-fair-use-in-american-society-for-testing-v-upc/)
- The Third Circuit **affirmed on Sept. 29, 2026**. It is described as the **first federal appellate decision on fair use in AI training**. Reported holdings: 2,243 headnotes are sufficiently original; factors 1, 3 and 4 weigh against fair use, and factor 2 slightly favours Ross; the use was "minimally transformative, at best"; harm was found both in the legal-research market and in a "rapidly developing" market for licensing headnotes as AI training data. — [IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2026/09/30/third-circuit-affirms-revised-fair-use-ruling-against-ross-ai-legal-research-platform-in-sealed-opinion/); [Jackson Walker](https://www.jw.com/news/insights-ai-copyright-fair-use-case/); [Haynes Boone](https://www.haynesboone.com/news/alerts/third-circuit-rejects-fair-use-for-rosss-copying-of-westlaw-headnotes); [Ballard Spahr](https://www.ballardspahr.com/insights/alerts-and-articles/2026/10/third-circuit-addresses-fair-use-in-ai-training-but-leaves-generative-ai-questions-unresolved)
- UNCERTAIN: IPWatchdog describes the opinion as initially **sealed**, with a public one-page "AFFIRMED" order. Whether the full opinion is now public, and the panel composition and author, were not verified. Commentators uniformly stress that it **does not decide generative-AI training**. — [IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2026/09/30/third-circuit-affirms-revised-fair-use-ruling-against-ross-ai-legal-research-platform-in-sealed-opinion/); [BakerHostetler](https://www.bakerlaw.com/insights/what-thomson-reuters-v-ross-does-and-doesnt-say-now-about-fair-use-and-generative-ai/)

**Getty Images v. Stability AI (UK, [2025] EWHC 2863 (Ch))**
- Judgment of Joanna Smith J, **4 Nov. 2025**: Getty's **secondary infringement** claim (import and dealing in an "article" that is an "infringing copy") failed because the Stable Diffusion model weights do not store or reproduce the works. Getty had dropped its primary training and output claims during trial because training took place outside the UK. Trade mark infringement was found only for limited, historic watermark outputs. — [AIPPI](https://www.aippi.org/news/getty-images-v-stability-ai-uk-high-court-finds-no-secondary-copyright-infringement-in-landmark-genai-ruling/); [Faegre Drinker](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2025/11/first-major-judgment-on-how-uk-copyright-law-applies-to-artificial-intelligence)
- **Permission to appeal was granted to Getty on 16 Dec. 2025** on the "infringing copy" question, meaning whether an intangible model must contain a copy. Stability's request to appeal the TM findings was refused at first instance. — [IPKat](https://ipkitten.blogspot.com/2026/01/permission-to-appeal-granted-in-getty.html?m=0); [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/de/insights-and-events/insights/2026/01/next-steps-for-getty-v-stability-why-has-permission-to-appeal-been-granted); [Burges Salmon](https://www.burges-salmon.com/articles/102lydc/getty-images-v-stability-ai-getty-granted-permission-to-appeal)

**EU AI Act GPAI copyright/TDM obligations**
- Art. 53(1)(c)–(d) requires GPAI providers to adopt a copyright policy that respects Art. 4(3) DSM Directive TDM opt-outs and to publish a "sufficiently detailed summary" of training content using the Commission template. The obligations have applied since **2 Aug. 2025**. Commission enforcement powers, including fines, apply from **2 Aug. 2026**. Models placed on the market before Aug. 2025 must comply by **2 Aug. 2027**. — [EU Commission AI Act page](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai); [Mayer Brown (July 2026)](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/07/eu-ai-act-news-digital-omnibus-on-ai-new-guidance-on-risk-classification-gpai-and-transparency-obligations)
- The GPAI Code of Practice (copyright chapter, July 2025) is voluntary. It commits signatories to honour machine-readable opt-outs such as robots.txt, not to circumvent paywalls, and to exclude known piracy domains. Commentators describe it as "quasi-mandatory." — [AI Resources (Code summary)](https://ai-resources.eu/soft-law/ai-act-code-of-practice/); [Chambers Practice Guide EU 2026](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/ai-intellectual-property-2026/eu/trends-and-developments)
- The **Digital Omnibus on AI** (reported as Reg. (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 July 2026) mainly delays high-risk deadlines, for example Annex III to 2 Dec. 2027. I found **no source saying it changed the Art. 53 copyright duties**. The regulation number and dates are single-sourced. — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/07/eu-ai-act-news-digital-omnibus-on-ai-new-guidance-on-risk-classification-gpai-and-transparency-obligations); [Wharton AI lab](https://ai-analytics.wharton.upenn.edu/wharton-accountable-ai-lab/eu-ai-act-update-the-digital-omnibus-and-the-realities-of-ai-governance/)
- **Like Company v. Google (CJEU C-250/25)** is a Hungarian referral and the first CJEU case on genAI. It asks whether LLM training is reproduction, whether Art. 4 TDM applies, and whether chatbot outputs are reproduction or communication by the provider. It is pending, reportedly before the Grand Chamber. The AG opinion's timing is unconfirmed, and a ruling is expected in 2027. The European Copyright Society filed a comment. — [European Copyright Society comment (Mar. 2026)](https://europeancopyrightsociety.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/ecs-comment-on-the-like-company-reference.pdf); [Stibbe](https://www.stibbe.com/publications-and-insights/ai-copyright-claims-in-europe-key-considerations); [Bonadio, World IP 2026 (Essex repository)](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/43535/1/World%20Intellectual%20Property%20-%202026%20-%20Bonadio%20-%20Preserving%20balance%20in%20the%20EU%20digital%20single%20market%20How%20Like%20Company%20could.pdf)
- The European Parliament adopted a non-legislative resolution on copyright and genAI in 2026, and the Commission follow-up document is dated July 2026. I did not read its content. — [EP follow-up doc](https://data.europarl.europa.eu/distribution/doc/SP-2026-07-27-TA-10-2026-0066_en.pdf)

### Inferences
- The US doctrinal picture has split along two lines. One is *source legality*: piracy-sourced copies attract liability or settlement pressure (Bartz, Kadrey's remaining claim, Concord II). The other is *transformativeness and market harm* for training. Courts favour defendants on training when there is no output regurgitation and no proven market substitution. Ross shows that non-generative, directly competing uses lose.
- Ross's recognition of a "rapidly developing" licensing market as cognizable factor-four harm is the doctrinal hook that NYT/MDL plaintiffs will press. That makes "licensing markets as market harm" the single most consequential open question for 2027.
- The NYT/MDL SJ ruling, the Getty CoA appeal, the GEMA OLG appeal, and the CJEU's Like Company ruling are all likely to land in 2027. A student paper written now should frame these as pending and design its argument to survive either outcome.
- UK (Getty: the model is not a copy) and Germany (GEMA: memorisation in weights is reproduction) have reached arguably opposite conclusions on the same technical question. That is a ready-made comparative-law topic.

## Q2. How "hot" is AI copyright in the literature, and who are the leading scholars and key works?

### Takeaway
AI copyright is among the most heavily written-on topics in AI law. It has a recognisable canon and leading scholars publishing in top venues, including *Science* (2026). I found no bibliometric study comparing its volume with other AI-law topics, so "hottest" is an informed impression, not a measured fact.

### Cited Findings
- **Pamela Samuelson (Berkeley)**, with Abhishek Nagaraj and Christopher Jon Sprigman, "Market dilution, fair use, and generative AI," *Science* 394:275–277 (2026). It argues that the "market dilution" theory (from Kadrey dicta and the USCO Part 3) is ill-defined, and that whether dilution exists is an **empirical question** that is currently unproven. — [Science](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.ael4888); [Berkeley Haas News](https://newsroom.haas.berkeley.edu/research/is-training-ai-on-copyrighted-works-fair-use-scholars-say-market-dilution-theory-still-lacks-evidence/)
- Samuelson, Sprigman and co-authors filed comments in the USCO AI inquiry (2023). — [regulations.gov](https://downloads.regulations.gov/COLC-2023-0006-8854/attachment_1.pdf)
- **Matthew Sag**, "Copyright Safety for Generative AI," 61 Houston L. Rev. 295 (2023), a foundational piece on memorisation and "copyright safety" practices. His "Fairness and Fair Use in Generative AI" appeared in the PIJIP/TLS series. — [Houston Law Review](https://houstonlawreview.org/article/92126-copyright-safety-for-generative-ai.pdf); [American Univ. Digital Commons](https://digitalcommons.wcl.american.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1120&context=research)
- **Mark Lemley**, "How Generative AI Turns Copyright Upside Down," Science & Technology Law Review (2024). It argues that genAI shifts value from expression to prompts and ideas, which strains the idea/expression and substantial-similarity doctrines. — [Stanford Law PDF](https://law.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/2024-09-30_How-Gerative-AI-Turns-Copyright-Upside-Down.pdf). Also widely cited (background knowledge, not re-verified here): Lemley & Casey, "Fair Learning," 99 Tex. L. Rev. 743 (2021).
- **A. Feder Cooper & James Grimmelmann**, "The Files are in the Computer: On Copyright, Memorization, and Generative AI" (arXiv/Chicago-Kent L. Rev.). It argues that a model that has memorised training data is a "copy." The counter-paper "We Should Separate Memorization from Copyright" argues that legal copying should be located in outputs. — [arXiv 2404.12590](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12590); [Authors Alliance, "On Memorization" (May 2026)](https://www.authorsalliance.org/2026/05/19/on-memorization/)
- **Andres Guadamuz (Sussex)**, "Artificial Intelligence and Copyright," a chapter dated Dec. 2025 in *Law, Policy, and the Internet* (Bloomsbury, 2026). It covers both copyrightability and training plus the EU AI Act. He also runs the TechnoLlama blog. — [Private Law Theory listing](https://www.private-law-theory.org/?p=79277); [TechnoLlama](https://www.technollama.co.uk/2025/11/28)
- **Jane Ginsburg (Columbia)** is associated with an empirical study, "Readers Prefer Outputs of AI Trained on Copyrighted Books over Expert Human Writers," which Authors Alliance treats as factor-four (substitution) evidence. Authorship and date were NOT verified. — [Authors Alliance](https://www.authorsalliance.org/2026/05/19/on-memorization/)
- European scholarship: the European Copyright Society comment on Like Company (2026), and Enrico Bonadio on Like Company in *Journal of World Intellectual Property* (2026). — [ECS](https://europeancopyrightsociety.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/ecs-comment-on-the-like-company-reference.pdf); [Essex repository](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/43535/1/World%20Intellectual%20Property%20-%202026%20-%20Bonadio%20-%20Preserving%20balance%20in%20the%20EU%20digital%20single%20market%20How%20Like%20Company%20could.pdf)
- Economics and CS are converging on the topic, for example "The Economics of AI Training Data: A Research Agenda" (arXiv 2510.24990) and "Market Design for AI: Beyond the Copyright Binary" (arXiv 2606.12260). — [arXiv 2510.24990](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24990); [arXiv 2606.12260](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.12260)

### Inferences
- The literature is very large in the US fair-use framing (2023–2025 wave). The 2026 frontier has shifted toward (i) empirical market-harm evidence, (ii) memorisation and outputs, and (iii) EU/CJEU and comparative questions. Interdisciplinary venues (Science, arXiv economics papers) are now a significant part of the conversation.
- Practitioner output is extraordinarily dense: dozens of law-firm alerts per ruling and multiple live trackers. This helps feasibility but creates noise, and students must privilege primary sources.

### Gaps
- No bibliometric comparison was found (for example, SSRN or HeinOnline counts of AI-copyright articles versus AI liability, AI discrimination, or AI Act governance). The relative "hotness" claim is therefore qualitative. A student could run their own SSRN/Google Scholar count as a methods footnote.
- I did not verify specific 2025–2026 law-review articles by Lemley, Sag or Ginsburg. Their earlier canonical works are confirmed.

## Q3. Is the topic saturated, and what sub-questions remain genuinely open or under-studied (including gaps scholars name explicitly)?

### Takeaway
The generic question "is AI training fair use?" is saturated. Narrow, evidence-driven or comparative sub-questions remain open. Several are explicitly flagged by scholars or courts as unresolved: market dilution, the licensing market as factor-four harm, memorisation and outputs, the "infringing copy" status of models, opt-out effectiveness, and creator (versus intermediary) compensation in licensing deals.

### Cited Findings
- **Market dilution is unproven and needs empirical work.** Samuelson, Nagaraj and Sprigman say whether dilution exists is "an empirical question" and that the theory lacks a clear definition. They also note that hybrid human-AI works complicate measurement. — [Science](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.ael4888); [Haas News](https://newsroom.haas.berkeley.edu/research/is-training-ai-on-copyrighted-works-fair-use-scholars-say-market-dilution-theory-still-lacks-evidence/)
- **Licensing markets as factor-four harm (circularity problem).** The Third Circuit in Ross counted a "rapidly developing" AI-training licensing market as cognizable harm. Commentators note that this leaves generative-AI questions unresolved. — [Ballard Spahr](https://www.ballardspahr.com/insights/alerts-and-articles/2026/10/third-circuit-addresses-fair-use-in-ai-training-but-leaves-generative-ai-questions-unresolved); [Business Law Today](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/business-law-today/2026-september/thomson-reuters-v-ross-one-year-later/)
- **Licensing deals, empirically.** A 2025 research agenda documents 2020–2025 deals as a fragmented market. Of 24 major deals, **only 7 compensate original creators**, while 17 pay only intermediaries. — [arXiv 2510.24990](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24990)
- Brookings/TollBit data: publishers with direct AI licensing deals initially had a click-through advantage, but by Q4 2025 this "deal premium" had essentially disappeared amid a six-fold CTR collapse. A Center for Journalism & Liberty report (April 2026) finds that deals compensate corporate rightsholders, not individual creators. — [Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/same-gatekeepers-new-tollbooths-in-the-ai-content-licensing-market/); [CJL report PDF](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5e449c8c3ef68d752f3e70dc/t/69f8a13537aba03b353607d6/1777901877061/Same+Gatekeepers+New+Tollbooths+-+Mapping+the+AI+Content+Licensing+Market+Report+April+2026+WEB.pdf)
- Commercial deal trackers disagree widely on deal counts (89 vs 312 deals), which shows there is **no authoritative dataset**. — [AI Industry Reviews](https://ai-industry-reviews.com/research/ai-training-data-licensing-deal-statistics-2026/); [Emet Research](https://emetresearch.framer.ai/blogs/market-report-ai-data-licensing-deals-(2020-present))
- **Memorisation and output-side infringement.** In Bartz and Kadrey, the absence of evidence that models regurgitated source works was decisive. That makes output memorisation the live frontier, and the doctrinal debate (Cooper & Grimmelmann vs. "Separate Memorization") is unresolved. — [Authors Alliance](https://www.authorsalliance.org/2026/05/19/on-memorization/); [Traverse Legal](https://www.traverselegal.com/blog/ai-copyright-memorization-model-outputs/)
- **Is a model a "copy"?** UK (Getty, under appeal on exactly this point) and Germany (GEMA: memorisation in weights is reproduction) diverge. — [Taylor Wessing on Getty appeal](https://www.taylorwessing.com/de/insights-and-events/insights/2026/01/next-steps-for-getty-v-stability-why-has-permission-to-appeal-been-granted); [Bird & Bird on GEMA](https://cm.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/landmark-ruling-of-the-munich-regional-court-(gema-v-openai)-on-copyright-and-ai-training)
- **EU TDM and outputs.** Like Company asks whether Art. 4 TDM even covers genAI training and whether chatbot outputs are reproduction by the provider. The ECS comment shows academic disagreement. — [ECS comment](https://europeancopyrightsociety.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/ecs-comment-on-the-like-company-reference.pdf)
- **AI-assisted authorship threshold.** Left open by Thaler and being litigated in Allen v. Perlmutter. — [Sterne Kessler](https://www.sternekessler.com/news-insights/client-alerts/ip-hot-topic-cert-denied-but-one-thing-is-cert-ain-the-human-authorship-requirement-for-copyright/)
- **Settlement design.** The Bartz release excludes outputs and future conduct, so settlement-as-licensing and the reach of releases are under-studied. One blog claims the $1.5B equals 44% of the 2025 training-data market. This is unverified, so do not cite it without checking. — [TAA blog](https://blog.taaonline.net/2026/07/bartz-v-anthropic-granted-final-approval-by-the-court/)

### Inferences
Promising, under-studied student-paper angles, ranked by feasibility:
1. **Ross and the licensing-market argument**: does recognising a nascent AI licensing market as factor-four harm create circularity (Campbell, Am. Geophysical Union v. Texaco, Ross)? Using the 2025 deal datasets as evidence is novel, tractable and timely.
2. **"Is the model a copy?" in a UK–Germany–EU comparison** (Getty, GEMA, Like Company): a clean comparative design with public judgments in English or with translations.
3. **Effectiveness of EU opt-outs**: Art. 4(3) DSM plus the AI Act Art. 53 and the Code of Practice. The question is whether robots.txt and TDM reservation protocols actually work. Pair with existing CS measurement studies of robots.txt adoption. I did not retrieve these in this pass, so the student would need to find them.
4. **Piracy as the de facto liability rule**: Bartz, Kadrey's distribution claim and Concord II show the US system penalising acquisition rather than training. Is that a coherent policy outcome?
5. **Class settlements as private licensing regimes** (Bartz terms, release scope, fee rulings).
6. **Creator vs. intermediary compensation in licensing deals**, building on the 7-of-24 finding.

Topics to avoid as too saturated: generic "is AI training fair use?" and "can AI be an author?" (the latter is effectively closed for fully autonomous works after the Thaler cert denial).

### Gaps
- I did not locate a formal literature review that explicitly enumerates "research gaps" in legal scholarship. The gaps listed are drawn from the Samuelson et al. Science piece, the economics research agenda, and commentator notes on Ross and Kadrey.
- I did not retrieve empirical CS studies on opt-out and robots.txt compliance (for example, the Data Provenance Initiative's "Consent in Crisis," which is from background knowledge and was not verified here).

## Q4. Feasibility for a student research paper using public sources

### Takeaway
Highly feasible. Nearly all key primary materials are public and free: US opinions and briefs on CourtListener, Getty and GEMA judgments, USCO reports, AI Act text and the Code of Practice, the CJEU docket, and SSRN/arXiv scholarship. The main risk is the moving target, since major rulings are expected in 2027. Students must scope narrowly and date-stamp their claims.

### Cited Findings
- SJ motions and other filings in the OpenAI MDL are freely mirrored (ChatGPTiseatingtheworld, Authors Guild), and Kadrey filings are on CourtListener RECAP. — [OpenAI SJ motion PDF](https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/OpenAI-SJ-Mtn-News-Cases-gov.uscourts.nysd_.612697.1496.0.pdf); [CourtListener Kadrey docket](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.415175/gov.uscourts.cand.415175.658.0.pdf); [govinfo Kadrey](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-cand-3_23-cv-03417/pdf/USCOURTS-cand-3_23-cv-03417-38.pdf)
- The USCO maintains fair-use case summaries, including Kadrey. — [copyright.gov](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/Kadrey-v-Meta-Platforms-Inc-788-F-Supp-3d-1028-ND-Cal-2025.pdf)
- The GEMA judgment and summaries are on WIPO Lex, and the Kadrey order is also on WIPO Lex. — [WIPO Lex GEMA](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/600712); [WIPO Lex Kadrey](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/600751)
- Multiple free trackers exist: ChatGPTiseatingtheworld, Mishcon, CMS, Hogan Lovells, and ailawsuittracker. Their accuracy varies. — [Mishcon](https://www.mishcon.com/generative-ai-intellectual-property-cases-and-policy-tracker); [Hogan Lovells](https://digital-client-solutions.hoganlovells.com/resources/ai-litigation-case-law-tracker/case/getty-images-us-inc-v-stability-ai-ltd/decision/71); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/aut/publication/artificial-intelligence-and-copyright-case-tracker/united-kingdom-getty-images-us-inc-ors-v-stability-ai-ltd3)

### Inferences
- Barriers: (1) the Ross appellate opinion may be partly sealed; (2) German-language sources for GEMA, although translations and summaries exist; (3) paywalled Law360 and Bloomberg Law for some docket developments; (4) the volume of low-quality AI-generated "tracker" sites. In this research, some trackers gave conflicting deal and settlement counts, so students should cite courts and reputable outlets.
- Recommended framing: write on a question that the pending 2027 rulings (NYT/MDL SJ, Getty CoA, GEMA OLG, CJEU Like Company) will *test* rather than *answer*. For example, "what evidence should count as licensing-market harm?" stays relevant whichever way those cases come out.
