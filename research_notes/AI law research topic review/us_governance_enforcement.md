## Q1. Verified current status (as of Nov 2026): EO 14365, DOJ suits against states, Colorado AI Act, SB 53, Texas TRAIGA, Illinois HB 3773, and congressional preemption

### Takeaway
EO 14365 (signed Dec 11, 2025; 90 Fed. Reg. 58499, Dec 16, 2025) set up a DOJ AI Litigation Task Force (Jan 9, 2026). As of this research, the federal government's only court action against a state AI law is DOJ's April 24, 2026 intervention in xAI's suit against Colorado. DOJ had not filed a standalone suit. Colorado then repealed and replaced its 2024 AI Act with the much narrower SB 26-189 (signed May 14, 2026; effective Jan 1, 2027; enforcement depends on AG rulemaking). California SB 53 and Texas TRAIGA took effect Jan 1, 2026, and no public enforcement action under either was found. Congress has still not enacted preemption.

### Cited Findings

**EO 14365 ("Ensuring a National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence")**
- Signed December 11, 2025 and published in the Federal Register December 16, 2025. The White House PDF is paginated "Presidential Documents 58499", i.e. 90 FR 58499 — [White House PDF](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/eo-14365.pdf); [Firewall blog](https://www.thefirewall-blog.com/2026/03/the-curious-case-of-executive-order-14365s-impact-on-ai-regulation/)
- Structure:
  - Sec. 4: Commerce must evaluate state AI laws within 90 days and identify "onerous" laws that conflict with federal policy.
  - Sec. 5(a): NTIA must issue a BEAD Policy Notice making states with onerous AI laws ineligible for non-deployment funds "to the maximum extent allowed by Federal law."
  - Sec. 7: the FTC must issue a policy statement on how Section 5 applies to AI models, including when state laws requiring altered "truthful outputs" are preempted.
  - Sec. 1 frames state rules as impermissibly regulating interstate commerce.
  - Sources: [EO text (Carahsoft mirror)](https://static.carahsoft.com/concrete/files/7617/7067/0934/Executive_Order_Executive_Order_14365_Ensuring_a_National_Policy_Framework_for_AI_1.pdf); [Phillips Lytle](https://phillipslytle.com/executive-order-issued-to-restrict-state-regulation-of-artificial-intelligence/); [Duane Morris](https://blogs.duanemorris.com/classactiondefense/2025/12/17/executive-order-signals-a-push-toward-a-single-federal-ai-rulebook-and-a-retreat-from-the-state-patchwork/)
- Commerce Sec. 4 evaluation: reported as not publicly released as of spring/late-May 2026. Secondary sources only, and I found no confirmation that it has since been published — [Baker Botts](https://ourtake.bakerbotts.com/post/102me4r/inside-the-dojs-new-ai-litigation-task-force); [Benesch](https://www.beneschlaw.com/insight/where-ai-regulation-stands-today/)
- FTC Sec. 7: Holland & Knight reports the FTC issued a *proposed* policy statement on July 1, 2026, with comments due July 31, 2026. The final status is unverified — [Holland & Knight, Aug 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/a-look-at-the-ftcs-current-approach-to-ai-regulation)
- One secondary summary wrongly says DOJ (rather than Commerce) identifies "onerous" laws. The primary text gives that job to Commerce — [Benesch](https://www.beneschlaw.com/insight/where-ai-regulation-stands-today/) vs. [White House PDF](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/eo-14365.pdf)
- Commentators across the spectrum agree that an EO cannot itself preempt state law. Under the Supremacy Clause, only a federal statute (or valid regulation) does that — [Firewall blog](https://www.thefirewall-blog.com/2026/03/the-curious-case-of-executive-order-14365s-impact-on-ai-regulation/); [CASRAI](https://casrai.org/news/federal-ai-moratorium-state-preemption-fight-2026)

**DOJ AI Litigation Task Force and suits against states**
- Established by Attorney General memorandum on January 9, 2026 — [Baker Botts](https://ourtake.bakerbotts.com/post/102me4r/inside-the-dojs-new-ai-litigation-task-force); [Consilium](https://consilium.law/sparkpoint/doj-ai-litigation-task-force/)
- A Substack commentary from about April 2026 noted that the Task Force was three months old and had "filed nothing." This is blog-level, but consistent with the rest of the record — [Regulating AI Substack](https://regulatingai.substack.com/p/the-doj-ai-litigation-task-force)
- **xAI v. Colorado (D. Colo.):**
  - xAI sued April 9, 2026, raising First Amendment (compelled speech; content/viewpoint discrimination), dormant Commerce Clause, vagueness, and Equal Protection claims.
  - DOJ moved to intervene April 24, 2026. Its theory is Equal Protection: the law requires prevention of *unintentional disparate impact* by race/sex and exempts "diversity"-promoting uses.
  - Sources: [DOJ press release (snippet only; fetch blocked)](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-intervenes-xai-lawsuit-challenging-colorados-algorithmic-discrimination); [Jenner & Block](https://www.jenner.com/en/news-insights/client-alerts/doj-joins-xai-in-lawsuit-challenging-colorado-ai-act); [NAA](https://naahq.org/news/doj-intervenes-case-against-algorithmic-discrimination-law); [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/united-states/litigation-mediation-arbitration/doj-intervenes-in-lawsuit-challenging-colorado%E2%80%99s-%E2%80%98algorithmic-discrimination%E2%80%99-law)
- Commentary notes that DOJ's intervention release does not mention the Task Force, and that this was the first federal action "on the ground" under EO 14365 — [Firewall blog](https://www.thefirewall-blog.com/2026/03/the-curious-case-of-executive-order-14365s-impact-on-ai-regulation/); [secureprivacy.ai (blog)](https://secureprivacy.ai/blog/trump-ai-executive-order-and-state-law-preemption-what-to-do-now)
- **Conflict on the stay:**
  - One aggregator says the court stayed enforcement on April 27, 2026 — [search summary of secureprivacy/ailawsbystate blogs](https://www.ailawsbystate.com/blog/federal-ai-preemption-doj-task-force-state-law-fight-2026).
  - Another says a magistrate judge granted a *joint* motion staying enforcement on August 28, 2026 — [WION](https://www.wionews.com/world/colorado-s-ai-discrimination-law-is-suspended-and-the-justice-department-helped-make-it-happen-1788077735194); [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/319420/20260701/colorado-ai-law-reset-discrimination-duty-dropped-disclosure-takes-its-place.htm).
  - Unresolved. The D. Colo. docket must be checked.
- **Unverified (single source):** a Lexology item says a later order, "EO 14434" dated September 29, 2026, folds the AI Litigation Task Force into a new DOJ "super intelligence task force" (SITF). I could not corroborate it, so it should not be relied on without the Federal Register text — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=39da867c-06d2-49a1-ad4b-e4abb32fb14d)
- I found no reporting of a DOJ-filed suit against California, Illinois, New York or any other state. Forecasts of a first suit "by Labor Day/Q4 2026" are predictions only — [modeldiplomat](https://modeldiplomat.com/story/state-ai-laws-mid-2026-trumps-push); [ailawsbystate](https://www.ailawsbystate.com/blog/federal-ai-preemption-doj-task-force-state-law-fight-2026)

**Colorado AI Act (SB 24-205) → SB 26-189**
- SB 24-205's effective date had been pushed to June 30, 2026. SB 26-189 repealed and replaced it, and Gov. Polis signed it May 14, 2026. It is 2026 Session Laws ch. 131, C.R.S. 6-1-1701 to -1709 — [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2026/05/colorado-ai-act-repeal-new-transparency-law); [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en-us/knowledge/publications/18733d31/colorado-enacts-revised-ai-law); [Finnegan](https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/articles/colorado-replaces-landmark-ai-act-an-overview-of-the-new-sb-26-189-framework.html)
- Substance of the change:
  - **Eliminated:** the duty of care against algorithmic discrimination, risk-management programs, impact assessments, annual reviews, and AG reporting.
  - **Now:** a transparency law. Deployers give pre-use disclosure, explain adverse outcomes within 30 days, and offer meaningful human review "where commercially reasonable."
  - **Enforcement:** exclusively by the AG under the Colorado Consumer Protection Act.
  - Sources: [Eckert Seamans](https://www.eckertseamans.com/legal-updates/colorado-repeals-and-replaces-its-landmark-ai-statute-what-businesses-need-to-know); [Buchalter](https://www.buchalter.com/insights/colorado-rewrites-its-ai-law-what-employers-must-know-about-sb-26-189/); [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/colorados-new-ai-act-targets-automated-decision-making-consequential-decisions)
- Effective January 1, 2027. The AG has said he will not enforce SB 24-205 or its replacement until rulemaking concludes, and rulemaking had not formally begun at the time of those alerts. Some coverage calls the new law the "Automated Decision-Making Technology Act" and says enforcement is on hold pending the xAI litigation — [DWT](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2026/05/colorado-ai-act-repeal-new-transparency-law); [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/319420/20260701/colorado-ai-law-reset-discrimination-duty-dropped-disclosure-takes-its-place.htm)

**California SB 53 (Transparency in Frontier AI Act)**
- Core obligations effective January 1, 2026. The California AG has exclusive enforcement authority, with civil penalties up to $1,000,000 per violation — [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20251001-transparency-in-frontier-artificial-intelligence-act-sb-53-california-requires-new-standardized-ai-safety-disclosures); [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251001-california-enacts-ai-safety-transparency-regulation-tfaia-sb-53); [Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/11/alerts-technology-aiml-california-moves-to-regulate-frontier-ai-with-a-focus-on-catastrophic-risk); [bill text, LegiScan](https://legiscan.com/CA/text/SB53/id/3270002)
- No reported SB 53 enforcement action was found. One blog claims enforcement is "paused" because of the preemption fight, but no other source corroborates this and it is probably wrong — [secureprivacy.ai](https://secureprivacy.ai/blog/california-sb-53-frontier-ai-transparency-act-explained-2026)
- Blog trackers list SB 53 as "high preemption risk," but no federal suit against it has been reported — [secureprivacy.ai](https://secureprivacy.ai/blog/trump-ai-executive-order-and-state-law-preemption-what-to-do-now)

**Texas TRAIGA (HB 149)**
- Effective January 1, 2026.
  - Enforcement is exclusively by the AG, with no private right of action.
  - The AG must give written notice and a 60-day cure period before suing.
  - The AG online complaint mechanism was due by September 1, 2026 and reportedly launched that day.
  - Sources: [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/c6c60e0c/the-texas-responsible-ai-governance-act); [CSA research note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-texas-traiga-enforcement-portal-20260913-c/)
- A tracker says that as of August 31, 2026 (rechecked September 29, 2026), no TRAIGA enforcement action or CID had been publicly reported. It notes that CIDs are not necessarily announced. This is a blog-level tracker — [traiga.news](https://traiga.news/traiga-enforcement-what-the-texas-ag-has-and-has-not-done-and-what-comes-next/)

**Congressional preemption attempts**
- The Senate voted 99–1 in July 2025 to strip a 10-year state-AI-law moratorium from the reconciliation bill (One Big Beautiful Bill Act).
  - A November 2025 attempt to add preemption to the NDAA also failed.
  - The White House sent legislative recommendations for a national framework on March 20, 2026.
  - A House discussion draft (three-year preemption of state AI-development rules) had stalled since June 2026.
  - No comprehensive federal AI statute was enacted as of October 2026.
  - Sources: [CASRAI](https://casrai.org/news/federal-ai-moratorium-state-preemption-fight-2026); [Ropes & Gray, Mar 2026](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/03/the-white-house-legislative-recommendations-national-policy-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-an); [Broadband Breakfast](https://broadbandbreakfast.com/ai-preemption-battle-lands-in-congress-with-substantive-discussion-draft/)
- Pending bills include H.R. 5388 (American AI Leadership and Uniformity Act) and H.R. 8037 (Protect American AI Act of 2026) — [Congress.gov H.R.5388](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/5388/text); [Congress.gov H.R.8037](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/8037)
- More than 200 state lawmakers and labor groups urged Congress in July 2026 to reject preemption — [CASRAI](https://casrai.org/news/federal-ai-moratorium-state-preemption-fight-2026)

### Inferences
- The federal preemption campaign has so far worked mainly through **pressure and litigation leverage, not legal displacement**. Colorado repealed its duty-of-care model under a pending federal-backed suit, and no court has ruled on the merits. Pressure working where courts have not ruled is itself a researchable thesis: preemption by "regulatory chill."
- DOJ chose an Equal Protection theory, attacking disparate-impact duties, rather than the dormant Commerce Clause theory the EO foregrounds. This suggests the administration's strongest legal hook is anti-disparate-impact policy, not interstate-commerce doctrine. That links preemption directly to the employment and algorithmic-discrimination topic.
- **Hotness: very high.** This is the most-discussed area in 2026 commentary. Primary sources are public (EO text, dockets, session laws, Congress.gov), which makes it workable for a student paper. The risk is the fast-moving status: the Commerce list, the xAI ruling and the possible EO 14434 could change things mid-paper.

### Gaps
- Exact procedural posture of *xAI v. Colorado* (case number, whether stayed in April or August, whether mooted or amended after SB 26-189). Needs a PACER/CourtListener check.
- Whether Commerce's Sec. 4 list, the NTIA BEAD notice, and a *final* FTC Sec. 7 policy statement have issued. No primary confirmation found.
- Existence and content of "EO 14434" (Sept 29, 2026). Single unverified source. Check the Federal Register.
- **Illinois HB 3773** (amending the Illinois Human Rights Act on AI in employment; from background knowledge, effective Jan 1, 2026). It was not verified in this session, and none of my searches returned it. Verify on ilga.gov (Public Act 103-0804) and check whether IDHR rules or any charges have been reported.
- Exact text of the justice.gov release and the NY Comptroller audit. Hosts were blocked, so the details come from snippets.

## Q2. What enforcement has actually occurred under AI-specific state laws vs. existing FTC/SEC/consumer-protection authority? Any counts or datasets?

### Takeaway
No public enforcement action was found under any AI-specific *state* statute (SB 53, TRAIGA, Colorado). NYC Local Law 144 enforcement has been minimal. Virtually all real AI enforcement 2023–2026 has run through general law:
- FTC Section 5 (Operation AI Comply, partly walked back in 2025)
- SEC antifraud ("AI washing": a handful of cases)
- State UDAP laws (e.g., Texas AG v. Pieces, 2024)

No authoritative public dataset of AI enforcement actions was identified. Counts vary by definition.

### Cited Findings
- **FTC Operation AI Comply** (September 2024) was a sweep including DoNotPay and Rytr — [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/241008-ftc-first-ai-related-enforcement-actions)
- **Rytr reversal:**
  - On December 22, 2025, the FTC (Chair Ferguson and Commissioner Meador, the only two sitting commissioners) reopened and set aside the 2024 Rytr consent order.
  - It cited the July 2025 AI Action Plan's direction to set aside orders that "unduly burden AI innovation," and found the alleged facts did not support a Section 5 violation. Rytr consented.
  - Ferguson had dissented from the original order.
  - Sources: [Rytr Order (ftc.gov)](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Rytr-Order.pdf); [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/in-rare-move-ftc-sets-aside-rytr-order-for-burdening-ai-innovation-and-failing-to-plead-violations); [Mintz](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/54731/2026-02-13-emerging-federal-ai-strategy-ftc-sets-aside-rytr); [Broadband Breakfast](https://broadbandbreakfast.com/ftc-vacates-consent-order-against-ai-company-rytr/)
- The current FTC still pursues deception about AI *capabilities* (DoNotPay, which Ferguson supported), but has dropped theories that treat AI tools themselves as harmful. The AI Action Plan directs review of prior-administration investigations — [Holland & Knight, Aug 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/a-look-at-the-ftcs-current-approach-to-ai-regulation); [Wiggin](https://www.wiggin.com/publication/ftc-sets-aside-rytr-llc-final-order-citing-the-trump-administrations-ai-action-plan/)
- The FTC issued fake-review warning letters in December 2025, relevant to AI-generated reviews — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-be/insights/publications/2025/12/ftc-warning-letters-ai-consumer-reviews)
- **Algorithmic disgorgement:** the FTC remedy of deleting models trained on unlawfully obtained data, first used in Cambridge Analytica (2019). In July 2023 an FTC official called it a "significant part" of FTC AI strategy — [Mintz PDF](https://www.mintz.com/mintz/pdf?id=92221); [Holland & Knight 2023](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2023/11/2023-artificial-intelligence-regulation-an-update-from-a-senior-ftc-official)
- **SEC "AI washing":**
  - First cases were Delphia and Global Predictions (March 2024). Presto Automation settled in January 2025 without a fine.
  - Reportedly no AI-focused SEC actions in the first half of 2026.
  - Three AI-related actions since late August 2026, including a settled fraud case against "GenesisAI" (August 26, 2026) and trading-bot scams.
  - Sources: [DLA Piper, Oct 2026](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/10/three-new-sec-cases-show-continued-scrutiny-of-ai-washing-claims); [Global Investigations Review 2026](https://globalinvestigationsreview.com/review/the-investigations-review-of-the-americas/2026/article/us-enforcement-agencies-intensify-scrutiny-of-ai-washing); [Quartz: "SEC warns ... but rarely punishes"](https://qz.com/sec-ai-washing-enforcement-small-companies-investors-100826)
- Count conflict: one tracker reportedly lists 12 AI-washing/securities actions, 7 active or pending. Other commentators describe fewer, narrower cases. The definition drives the number — [promise.legal blog](https://blog.promise.legal/ai-washing-compliance-sec-ftc-state-enforcement/); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/10/three-new-sec-cases-show-continued-scrutiny-of-ai-washing-claims)
- **State AG under general UDAP law, Texas AG v. Pieces Technologies:**
  - Announced September 18, 2024 under the Texas DTPA. The AG alleged inaccurate "severe hallucination rate" and accuracy claims for a clinical generative-AI tool.
  - Resolved by an assurance of voluntary compliance, with no monetary payment. Pieces denied wrongdoing.
  - Described as a "first-of-its-kind" healthcare generative-AI settlement.
  - Sources: [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/texas-attorney-general-announces-first-its-kind-healthcare-generative-ai-settlement); [Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2024/12/alerts-practices-hltc-texas-ag-enters-into-settlement-with-provider-of-gen-ai-tools); [Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2024/12/rising-ai-enforcement-insights-from-state-attorney-general-settlement-and-us-ftc-sweep)
- **AI-specific statutes, enforcement found:** none under SB 53 or TRAIGA (Q1). For NYC LL144, see Q3.

### Inferences
- The empirical pattern is stark. AI-specific statutes have generated close to zero public enforcement, while the actual sanctions come from decades-old deception and antifraud law. This supports the scholarly claim that existing law already reaches most AI harms. It also shows the limit: existing law reaches *misrepresentation about AI*, not discriminatory or unsafe AI per se, and the 2025–26 FTC has explicitly narrowed to that.
- **Regulatory volatility:** the Rytr set-aside is a rare instance of an agency vacating its own consent order on policy grounds. It is a concrete, citable hook for papers on the durability of "regulation by enforcement."
- **Strong student-paper opportunity:** build an original, hand-coded dataset of AI-related enforcement actions (FTC, SEC, state AGs, 2023–2026) from public press releases. No authoritative dataset was found, so this would be a genuine contribution.

### Gaps
- No authoritative, methodologically documented dataset of US AI enforcement actions was found. Commercial trackers (e.g., airiskaware.com, promise.legal) exist but are unvetted.
- Total count of Operation AI Comply and subsequent FTC AI cases was not compiled here. Other cases recalled from background (Ascend Ecom, Ecommerce Empire Builders, FBA Machine, Rite Aid facial recognition 2023, Evolv, IntelliVision, Workado, Air AI) were not verified in this session.
- Other state AG AI actions (e.g., Massachusetts, New Jersey, multistate letters on AI chatbots and children) were not researched for lack of budget.
- The SEC "GenesisAI" case name and details come from a single law-firm alert.

## Q3. Empirical research on NYC Local Law 144 compliance and similar

### Takeaway
The leading empirical study is Wright et al., "Null Compliance" (FAccT 2024). It found only 18 of 391 employers (about 5%) posted bias audits and 13 (about 3%) posted transparency notices, and it argued that the law's design makes non-compliance unobservable. The NY State Comptroller's December 2025 audit found DCWP's enforcement "ineffective." Together they make LL144 the best-documented case study of a US AI-specific law failing in implementation.

### Cited Findings
- **Wright, L., et al., "Null Compliance: NYC Local Law 144 and the Challenges of Algorithm Accountability," ACM FAccT 2024 (arXiv 2406.01399).**
  - Method: student investigators posing as job seekers checked 391 employers' websites.
  - Result: affirmative compliance was 18 bias audits (5%) and 13 transparency notices (3%).
  - Key concept: because employers self-determine whether their tool is in scope, researchers can measure compliance but not non-compliance. The authors call this "null compliance."
  - Sources: [FAccT paper PDF](https://facctconference.org/static/papers24/facct24-113.pdf); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.01399v1)
- **NY State Comptroller (DiNapoli) audit of NYC DCWP**, December 2025 (report sga-2026-24n6), covering July 2023 to June 2025.
  - Found DCWP's enforcement system "ineffective." DCWP reviewed 32 employer/vendor websites and found only one instance of noncompliance, and auditors judged the review inadequate.
  - DCWP disputed some findings but agreed to adopt most recommendations in part or in full.
  - Sources: [OSC press release](https://www.osc.ny.gov/press/releases/2025/12/dinapoli-new-yorkers-deserve-transparent-hiring-process-when-artificial-intelligence-used-vet-their); [audit PDF](https://www.osc.ny.gov/files/state-agencies/audits/pdf/sga-2026-24n6.pdf); [DLA Piper, Jan 2026](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/01/critical-audit-of-nyc-ai-hiring-law-signals-increased-risk-for-employers); [Staffing Industry Analysts](https://www.staffingindustry.com/editorial/cws-30-contingent-workforce-strategies/enforcement-of-new-york-s-ai-in-hiring-law-falling-short); [DCI Consult](https://blog.dciconsult.com/recommended-changes-to-local-law-144)
  - Note: OSC pages could not be fetched directly, so the figures come from search excerpts and law-firm summaries.
- Practitioner blogs claim LL144 enforcement is "ramping up in 2026" after the audit. This is not verified with DCWP data — [stmicro blog](https://www.stmicro.net/blog/nyc-local-law-144-ai-hiring-compliance/)

### Inferences
- LL144 is the only US AI-specific law with roughly three years of operating history plus both academic and government audits. That gives it the richest evidence base for an implementation or effectiveness study.
- **Paper opportunity:** a replication or update of Wright et al. for 2026 (re-checking the same or a new employer sample for posted audits) is feasible with public websites and would be novel. So would a comparative "law-in-action" study of LL144, Illinois HB 3773 and Colorado SB 26-189 disclosure duties.

### Gaps
- Precise numbers of complaints DCWP received and penalties issued under LL144 were not obtained, because the OSC fetch was blocked.
- No empirical compliance study of Illinois's AI Video Interview Act, Illinois HB 3773, or Colorado was found. This is likely a true gap in the literature.
- Other empirical LL144 work, e.g. by Data & Society or by Groves et al. on auditing the auditors, was not located in this session. Background recall only.

## Q4. Leading scholarly arguments on preemption/federalism and AI, and on "regulation by enforcement"

### Takeaway
The federalism debate in 2025–26 is carried mainly by:
- Lawfare and Institute for Law & AI commentary (Rozenshtein, Ball, Bullock), arguing over *how* to preempt (development-level vs. use-level)
- David Rubenstein's "Federalism & Algorithms" (Arizona Law Review), which explicitly launches "AI federalism" as a research field
- Brookings work on constitutional constraints, which is skeptical that dormant Commerce Clause doctrine invalidates non-protectionist state AI laws

On enforcement-based governance, the canonical work is Selbst & Barocas on FTC unfairness authority over discriminatory AI, plus a newer business-law literature on AI washing (Ofir, American Business Law Journal 2026). Peer-reviewed law-review scholarship specifically on EO 14365 was **not** found. The literature is still mostly commentary.

### Cited Findings
- **David S. Rubenstein, "Federalism & Algorithms," Arizona Law Review.** Presents itself as an "inaugural study and research agenda" for "AI federalism" and discusses the 2025 moratorium fight — [Arizona L. Rev. PDF](https://journals.librarypublishing.arizona.edu/arizlrev/article/10287/galley/9161/download/)
- **Alan Rozenshtein and Dean Ball (Lawfare), "Congress Should Preempt State AI Safety Legislation."** Argues for preempting state rules on AI *model development* while leaving *use* regulation to states — [Lawfare](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/congress-should-preempt-state-ai-safety-legislation)

## Q5. Explicit research gaps identified by scholars, and suitability for a student paper

### Takeaway
Explicit gaps flagged in the sources:
- Rubenstein frames AI federalism as an undeveloped field needing a research agenda.
- Wright et al. show that self-scoping disclosure laws make non-compliance unmeasurable.
- Practitioners note no enforcement record yet exists for SB 53, TRAIGA or Colorado.

The strongest student topics are those that turn these gaps into tractable, public-source projects.

### Cited Findings
- Rubenstein explicitly offers a "research agenda" for AI federalism, signalling that the area is under-theorized — [Arizona L. Rev.](https://journals.librarypublishing.arizona.edu/arizlrev/article/10287/galley/9161/download/)
- Wright et al. identify the structural gap that researchers cannot measure non-compliance where employers self-determine coverage, and call for design changes to algorithm accountability laws — [FAccT 2024](https://facctconference.org/static/papers24/facct24-113.pdf)
- No TRAIGA enforcement or CID was publicly reported through late September 2026. CIDs are not necessarily public, which is a transparency gap for empirical study — [traiga.news](https://traiga.news/traiga-enforcement-what-the-texas-ag-has-and-has-not-done-and-what-comes-next/)
- The question in *Mobley* of whether bias-testing data is privileged raises an unresolved tension between auditing duties and litigation discovery — [Inside Tech Law](https://www.insidetechlaw.com/blog/2026/06/behind-the-privilege-shield-safeguarding-ai-bias-testing-data-in-employment-decisions)
- Commentators disagree on whether the FTC's 2025–26 shift is a "recalibration" or a wholesale abandonment of AI precedent. The empirical question of how many orders have been reopened is open — [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/in-rare-move-ftc-sets-aside-rytr-order-for-burdening-ai-innovation-and-failing-to-plead-violations); [Mondaq/Wiggin](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/new-technology/1743720/the-ftc-enters-new-chapter-in-its-approach-to-artificial-intelligence-and-enforcement)

### Inferences
**Topic ratings for an original student paper using public sources:**

1. **Federal preemption via executive action (EO 14365, Task Force, xAI v. Colorado). Strength: HIGH.**
   - Hot, under-theorized in formal scholarship, with public primary sources.
   - Possible theses: (i) "preemption without a statute," meaning executive pressure, funding conditions (BEAD) and litigation leverage as de facto preemption, with Colorado as the case study; (ii) Equal Protection versus disparate-impact duties in state AI law.
   - Risk: fast-moving facts. Lock a status cutoff date.

2. **State AI statutes (Colorado repeal, SB 53, TRAIGA, IL HB 3773). Strength: MEDIUM-HIGH.**
   - Best as comparative design analysis: duty-of-care/impact-assessment (old Colorado) vs. transparency (SB 26-189, SB 53) vs. intent-based prohibitions (TRAIGA).
   - "Why the Colorado model collapsed" is fresh.
   - Weakness: there is no enforcement record yet, so effectiveness claims are speculative.

3. **Algorithmic discrimination in employment. Strength: HIGH if narrowly framed.**
   - The general topic is crowded, but narrow angles are open: *Mobley* agent liability for vendors; the privilege-for-bias-audits question; the federal retreat from disparate impact vs. state laws (LL144, IL HB 3773, CA FEHA).
   - An LL144 compliance replication is a feasible original empirical contribution.

4. **Enforcement through existing law (FTC, SEC, state AGs). Strength: HIGH for empirical work.**
   - There is no authoritative public dataset. Hand-coding all FTC, SEC and state-AG AI actions 2023–2026 from press releases, and analysing theories, remedies and the 2025 reversal (Rytr), would be original.
   - Doctrinally, the narrowing to deception-only theories versus unfairness/discrimination theories (Selbst & Barocas) is a live debate.
