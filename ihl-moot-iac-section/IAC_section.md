**There are substantial grounds to believe that an International Armed Conflict (IAC) existed from about 15 March 2025**
*(bold, underlined, centred, black, like the Count 1 headings)*

Both counts require an international armed conflict; as neither the Statute nor the Elements defines it, the Geneva Conventions apply.¹ They cover armed conflict “between two or more of the High Contracting Parties”, such as Damaroi and Pallavon.² Any “resort to armed force between States” suffices,³ whatever its intensity or duration and even if unresisted.⁴

On 3 February, President Veyar tasked General Thorn “to dismantle Pallavon’s aerial defence systems” so that Damaroi could “take control of the country”.⁵ About 15 March, Thorn led 50 elite soldiers, the Titans, into Pallavon by stealth.⁶ Retreating on 19 March, he burned the Pallavon bank for “easier access next time”; five Titans sent ahead never returned.⁷ This unconsented deployment of Damaroi’s armed forces was a resort to armed force between States. Pallavon’s failure to detect it is irrelevant: classification depends on the facts, and the Head of State deliberately ordered it.⁸

IHL applies “in the whole territory of the warring States … whether or not actual combat takes place there” until “the general close of military operations”.⁹ The operation was paused, not closed: Thorn said “When we go back in, we need to be stronger”, and Operation Titan 2.0 re-entered on 1 June.¹⁰ Hostilities then escalated,¹¹ and peace talks began only after the Titans’ capture on 31 July.¹² The injections in Damaroi (2–10 April) and the 22 July strike thus both occurred within the IAC,¹³ which there are substantial grounds to believe existed throughout both counts.¹⁴ Nexus and awareness are argued under each count.
