## Key Patterns and Strategic Insights

### 1. The Title Formula
Almost every video follows: **"[Number] Most Disturbing [Topic]"**
- This is the backbone of the brand. It sets clear expectations and promises a list-based descent.
- Notable exceptions that ALSO performed: "The FBI's 10 Most Wanted Explained" (9.9M), "Conspiracy Theories That Turned Out to Be True" (7.2M), "Urban Legends That Turned Out to Be True" (6.7M).
- The exceptions that work tend to promise RESOLUTION ("turned out to be true," "explained," "solved by Reddit").

### 2. Visual Proof = Higher Ceiling
The pattern is unmistakable: videos built around **real footage** (dashcam, doorbell, forest camera, bodycam, live TV) dramatically outperform text-based content (Reddit threads, 4Chan). The top 6 outliers are ALL "caught on camera" videos.
- Dashcam series: 5-21M views
- Forest/camping caught on camera: 7-11M
- Reddit threads (no footage): 3-7M
- 4Chan threads (no footage): 2-6M

### 3. Duration Sweet Spot
- Best performers cluster around **15-25 minutes**
- Videos over 30 minutes still perform but show no advantage
- The 36-minute "Audio Recordings" video underperformed (1.4M) - suggests longer is not better
- Shorter videos (11-14 min) can pop if the topic is strong ("Outdoor YouTubers Who Didn't Return Home" at 12 min = 3.4M)

### 4. Series Iteration Works (With Diminishing Returns)
- Dashcam series: Vol 1 (13M) -> Vol 3 (21M peak) -> Vol 6 (12M). The series CAN grow, but eventually regresses.
- Reddit Threads: Maintains 2-7M across many installments. Remarkably stable.
- 4Chan Threads: 2-6M. Similar stability.
- Forest Encounters: 7.5-10M. Consistent.
- Key lesson: **Series work if the source material stays fresh.** Dashcam peaked at Vol 3 because the format had already shown everything it could. Reddit/4Chan endure because the source material is infinite.

### 5. Breadth of Appeal Determines Ceiling
- **Universal topics** (dashcam, doorbell camera, camping = things everyone encounters) -> 8-21M
- **Internet culture** (Reddit, 4Chan, rabbit holes = things internet-savvy audiences know) -> 3-10M
- **Niche true crime** (911 confessions, identity theft killers = requires specific interest) -> 600K-1.5M

### 6. Engagement Metrics
- **Like-to-view ratio**: 2-3% consistently (very strong for this niche)
- **Comment-to-view ratio**: 0.1-0.2% (healthy engagement)
- "The Horrifying Real Events Behind These Memes" hit 4% like ratio (104K likes on 2.6M views) - the highest engagement, suggesting the audience deeply values creative reframing of familiar content
- "5 Most Disturbing Cases of Real Found Footage" (June 2025) hit 159K likes on 8.7M views - the absolute like count leader

### 7. What Underperforms and Why
| Video | Views | Why It Struggled |
|---|---|---|
| 4 Most Disturbing 911 Confessions | 644K | Too narrow, no visual element, audio-only |
| Streamers Who Captured Their Final Moments | 805K | Sensitive + overlaps with existing "last moments" videos |
| Killers Who Stole Their Victims' Identity | 915K | Cerebral concept, no visceral hook |
| Goodbye Recordings from People Who Expected to Die | 1.0M | Emotionally heavy without the mystery element |
| 5 Most Disturbing Audio Recordings | 1.4M | Audio = no visual hook, longest video (37 min) |

**Common thread**: Videos fail when they lack either (a) a visual element, (b) mystery/curiosity, or (c) broad relatability.

## How This Channel Compares to the Abyssal Network

### What Chilling Scares Does That Abyssal Should Study:

1. **Relentless consistency** - Every 10-12 days, no exceptions. 37 videos in 12 months. The algorithm rewards this.

2. **Series thinking with volume tracking** - Dashcam Vol 1-6, multiple Reddit Threads installments. The audience knows what to expect and returns. Abyssal does icebergs, but Chilling Scares proves that repeating a winning format with fresh entries is not a weakness - it's a growth engine.

3. **Title simplicity** - "[Number] Most Disturbing [Topic]" is SEO-friendly, immediately parseable, and sets exact expectations. No cleverness getting in the way of the click.

4. **Real footage as the anchor** - The channel's biggest hits all have "caught on camera" or "footage" in the title. This signals PROOF to the viewer - they're not getting a narrated story, they're getting real evidence. Abyssal could benefit from more footage-anchored content.

5. **Topic breadth without brand dilution** - Chilling Scares covers Reddit, 4Chan, dashcams, forests, YouTube culture, memes, true crime, EAS alerts - wildly different territories - yet the brand never feels scattered because the wrapper is always the same: "Most Disturbing [X]."

6. **Resolution-based angles convert** - "Solved by Reddit," "Turned Out to Be True," "Explained" - these perform at or above the channel average. The audience wants the payoff, not just the descent.

### What Chilling Scares Does NOT Do (Potential Weaknesses):

1. **No iceberg/descent structure** - Every video is a flat list. Entry 1 and entry 5 could be swapped without losing anything. There's no escalation architecture. This is exactly what the Abyssal JD calls "a list maker" - Chilling Scares IS a list maker, and it works at scale, but it means the format has a ceiling on depth and viewer investment.

2. **No narrative arc** - Videos are compilations, not stories. Each segment is self-contained. This means high click-through but potentially lower watch-through on longer videos.

3. **Repetitive titling eventually saturates** - How many "5 Most Disturbing Reddit Threads" can you make before the audience stops distinguishing them? The channel mitigates this by sheer volume, but title fatigue is a real risk.

4. **No community layer / paid content** - No Patreon, no uncensored cuts, no membership. This is pure ad-revenue at scale. Abyssal's Patreon layer is a strategic advantage CS doesn't have.

5. **Verification unclear** - The descriptions are minimal ("These are 5 terrifying threads posted to Reddit"). There's no editorial standard communicated. Abyssal's verification gate is a differentiator for audience trust.

## Key Takeaways for Your Application

1. **Chilling Scares proves the floor**: A well-executed list format with consistent uploads can sustain 2-3M views per video and 450K sub growth/year. The Abyssal Network should be hitting these numbers or exceeding them given its superior format depth.

2. **The "caught on camera" insight**: Abyssal Detective 2 (disasters, accidents) sits directly in CS's strongest territory. If AD2's baseline is declining, it's worth examining whether the iceberg format is hurting a topic category that CS proves works better as a compilation.

3. **Breadth vs. depth tradeoff**: CS achieves breadth (many topics) at the cost of depth (no escalation). Abyssal achieves depth (iceberg descent) at the cost of breadth (three fixed territories). The winning strategy may be borrowing CS's topic diversity while maintaining Abyssal's structural superiority.

4. **Consistent cadence is non-negotiable**: CS uploads every 10-12 days. If Abyssal's 8 videos/month across three channels means some channels go quiet, that's a problem CS never has.
