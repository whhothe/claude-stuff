I got this wrong once: making the level mean the document reads beautifully and fails arithmetic — “they knew and did it anyway” is the rarest state there is, and the spec needs 5–7 cases at every tier.

AO — “the Most Disturbing Reality TV Casting Iceberg”

The document: the background check.  7 levels · ~42 cases, one person each · 50 min · anchors verified, inventory is week one
Why AO gets a different answer
Kitchen Nightmares died twice: inspection records are purged after ~3 years, and one show cannot fill seven levels — you need 5–7 cases at the deepest tier and it has one Cerniglia.
Hook
A casting tape. Bright, likeable, saying why he’d be great on the show. “Eleven months later he was arrested for murder. The network said it ran a full check. It did.”
The named-show objection
AO’s 777K-vs-50K gap was never about the name — Marvel failed because nothing was underneath. Every case here names a show and has a court record under it.

AD — “the Most Disturbing Structural Collapses Iceberg”

The document: the engineering report.  7 levels · ~44 cases · 55 min · anchors verified, inventory is week one — levels run on how long the warning sat
Why it beats a standard AD video
A footage iceberg promises a clip you haven’t seen. This promises the ceiling above your head — then tells you it was written down. No collapse iceberg exists.
Hook
40 seconds of a fixed security camera on an ordinary lobby. Then the frame fills with dust. “Nineteen months before this, an engineer wrote a report about this building. Somebody read it.”
Why it fits AD
Its disaster lane has done explosion, coaster, mountain, outdoor, water park, sea — never things built by people that fell while they were inside. Most-filmed disaster type there is.

AD2 — “the Most Disturbing Lift & Cable Failures Iceberg”

The document: the inspection certificate.  7 levels · ~40 cases · 48 min · anchors verified, inventory is week one — elevators, escalators, funiculars, cable cars, ski lifts, mine hoists
Why I widened it
Elevators alone: ~30 US deaths/yr, nearly half maintenance workers in shafts — almost never on camera. Footage is thickest at the shallow end and thinnest where the payoff lives.
Hook
“There is a certificate in your lobby with a date on it. You have never read it. Every person in this video was killed by a machine whose certificate had already expired.”
What the wider family fixes
Same document, far better footage, and visually distinct segment to segment — which clears your “segments too similar” killer that 40 elevators alone would have hit.
